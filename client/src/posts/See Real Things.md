Theo Jansen is a Dutch artist who studied physics at the Delft University of Tech. He left during his 3rd year of studies to pursue the creation of art.

His magnum opus is the [Strandbeest](https://www.strandbeest.com/). Strandbeests were created to help stop the consumption of nordic islands by the ocean, as tides creep higher and higher due to climate change. 

Jansen theorized that if he could come up with a way to create artificial sand dunes by displacing sand along the beach, then he would slow the loss of land to the ocean. This is the task that Strandbeests were invented to perform.

*Computa, …*

For most of my life I have been at odds with computers. I have struggled to understand what goes on between the click of a button, and the output of text on screen. My favourite programming language is C for this exact reason. C is a low level language. It gives the programmer more control over small things like how memory is allocated, or where memory is stored and how it is accessed between variables. I am terrible at coding, but C has made me a bit better. In being forced to understand not just *that* data moves, but actually *how* it moves, I have gotten a better conceptual understanding of how computers work.

C is not suitable for building projects that deal with large amounts of fast moving data, instructed to be “generally correct”. What C is good for is building systems that are very particular and that require attention to how every piece of the structure moves in relation to the whole. Sure, C might be slow, clunky and disorganized, but there is something so elegant about it and about a workflow that is not optimized for anything other than the complete control and understanding of the person wielding it.

*My hands…*

I am excellent at making things that start and finish with an object that I can touch and see. For example, I have this wooden ball that I love dearly. I made it on a lathe. It started out as a 1 foot by 1 foot block of oak wood. Something that I can interact with the entire time and whose fibers I can quite literally mold exactly to my liking. There is no outsource, no short cuts, no delete button. Every curve is completely deliberate. 

![The wooden ball](woodball.jpeg)

*Optimizing…*

After 3 years of studying engineering, the concept of optimization has become second nature, and even outside of class the world around me is optimized.

The predictable path here is to complain about social media algorithms, but I think that it goes deeper than that.

Optimization is an interpretation of perfection if perfection was defined as exactly what you want and how you want it, as soon as fucking possible.  

In theory, this makes sense and is something that people should strive towards, but I think it has some unbeknownst side effects when it is put into practice at a very large scale. 

*Blob blob…*

A couple of weeks ago I went to a friend's cottage. I haven’t spent time on a lake in a couple of years, and I was really excited to do some fishing, set some traps and just be in that sort of environment. 

I’m a pretty shitty fisherman, so over the whole weekend I only caught 1 bass, but that bass represented a lot to me. It reminded me of how much I take for granted on a daily basis, that food often just shows up in my fridge, or at my grocery store, and I never take into account the journey that it had to go on to get there. 

![riccardoaug](augrickyboat.jpg)

I’m not going to start preaching about aboriginal lands and paying respects, because I’m just not into that stuff, but I am into showing an appreciation for things that have literally given their life so that I can live. And I am very serious about the respect that should be given to animals, plants and nature itself for all that it gives us.

This fish (later named Herbert), was my way of re-connecting with that world. I had a suspicion that I had grown dull, my edge had worn off because I was too used to the lies being disguised as convenient optimization. I have become desensitized to things, numb to the process, careless with the how, and only concerned with the when. I can’t stand it anymore, so I’m going to walk out the door.

*Bing bong…*

This summer I got a job at a piano store, working half-time at the front desk, and the other half in the technician workshop, helping to rebuild and refurbish pianos. 

In late April I had this weird irk, where I felt disconnected from my hands, which is why I applied to this job. 

Building things with wood, power tools, glue, felt and sand paper, in the way that I used to when I was growing up in my dad’s workshop, made me feel grounded again. I got to see something go from A to B, and be able to understand how and why it does its job, no corners cut. 

![pianophoto](piano1.jpg)

It has been a long time since I carved the ball out of wood, and I was craving that raw interaction again.  

This job has taken me out of the twisting, confusing world of constant noise, speed and fakeness. It has brought me back to reality, reminded me to slow down and show myself what I can achieve with my hands. I am reminded of what it feels like to control something, rather than it controlling you. 

*Bring it around…*

I think this is why Jansen’s Strandbeests caught my attention. It has been so long since I have seen someone make something that is almost purposely unoptimized. 

Even though I have never seen one irl, Strandbeests feel real to me. In the same eerie way that Howl’s Moving Castle feels alive.

Strandbeests are made out of PVC pipe and zip ties, and when you watch them sail across beaches, you can see every part of the mechanism move in response to another. You can see how the machine works, and it makes you feel close to it.

To be clear, Jansen is a scientist, and so his inventions have iterations, and between iterations he is of course optimizing for specific attributes. For example, he invented a gait pattern specific to his Strandbeests, using millions of data points to optimize a walking pattern that would allow the Strandbeests to effectively perform their task of sand dune creation. 

I am not advocating for eradicating optimization, rather, I’m an advocate for the occasional, intentional, pessimization. 

If I were drunk, which I’m not, but if I was, I would tear off on a tangent about the future of engineering, my love for solar punk, and the auspicious, yet almost forbidden relationship that lies at this intersection “between” “art” and “engineering”. I might even venture to say that, in my wildest of dreams, the boundaries which permit the existence of this idea become so muddled that the aforementioned words become completely synonymous. I might suggest Jansen’s inventions to be a step in that direction. 

It is inspiring to see that in the world of bits and bytes and algorithmic who-gives-a-fucks, there is still a truth to be found in the creation of something that doesn’t immediately work. The discussion of form over function becomes a discussion of form and function, as they are not mutually exclusive.

*Aaaaand sooooo…*

I feel a great disconnect between myself and the world when I take a process for granted. Optimization is great, speed is important and effectiveness is critical, but there is an irony here. Think about a product. In order to optimize a product, you must fully understand it. But , when you bring someone that fully optimized product, there is a function of desensitization that occurs as a result of them not really understanding what they have been given. 

They have not seen this product from start to finish, in all its flaws and glory. They have not seen it swim, flounder, flap and flail. They did not know what it looked like before it was a sphere. They do not know the thousands of iterations that came before this perfection.

That is what good engineering is. It breaks down the friction between the process and the outcome, which allows a customer to enjoy the benefits without the struggle. But the short coming is that, to them, this product merely exists. Sure, that is enough to have a shallow, user-usee relationship with it, but they are not bound to it. 

Does it not go deeper?

I think there is some middle ground here, I think uncovering it might be worthwhile.
